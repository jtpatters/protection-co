For parents and players

Is Protection Co right for you?

Protection Co is a business game about running a car insurance company. It has no violence, no bad language and nothing to buy. Because it is about insurance, it is full of the things insurance pays for: car accidents, storms and floods. This page lists everything a player sees and hears, so you can decide for yourself.

In short

Fine for all ages, written for teens and adults

Nothing in the game is graphic or frightening, and nobody is ever shown hurt. It is a game of reading and numbers, though: prices, claims, profit and capital. Younger children may find it hard going and may want an adult to play alongside them.

What you will see and hear

Car accidents

The town on the map has traffic, and now and then two cars collide at a crossroads, or several pile up on the highway. You see the dented cars, hear the crash and the car horns, and watch an ambulance and a fire truck arrive with their sirens on. Nobody is shown hurt, and there is no blood.

The town paper reports on the quarter's crashes, sometimes saying a crash ended in the hospital. Accidents are always told as what they cost the company in claims, never as anyone's injuries.

Storms, hail and snowstorms

Some quarters bring bad weather: the sky darkens, rain or hail falls, the wind bends the trees, and there is thunder and lightning. In winter a storm is a snowstorm. Bad weather means a run of claims, and the game is about whether the company has saved enough to pay them.

Floods

In the towns by the water, Millbrook and Harborview, a storm can flood the streets. You see the water rise between the buildings and then drain away. Nobody is shown in danger.

Flashing light

During a thunderstorm, lightning lights up the whole map in a quick double flash every few seconds. Police cars on the map have flashing lights on their roofs, and one desk situation shows a bright flash where a meteorite lands. If flashing light is a concern for you, keep this in mind: the iPhone's Reduce Motion setting does not turn the lightning off.

Sound

The game has music and town sounds, including thunder, hail, car crashes, horns and sirens. Tap Sound at the top right of the map to turn all of it off.

Dishonest offers, and the police

Things land on the company's desk that call for a decision, and some are about honesty. A body shop offers a secret payment for sending it repair work. A customer's claim does not add up. An agent is called for jury duty on a fraud trial. The player chooses what to do. Dishonest choices can come back later, and if the player takes the body shop's money, the police raid the shop: police cars arrive on the map and the paper reports it.

Choices that rely on chance

Some answers on the desk have a stated chance of working out, such as "probably" or "with some luck", which the player weighs before choosing. There is no betting, no casino, no loot boxes and no real money. All the money in the game is pretend.

Running a business

The player hires staff, and can also let them go. Staff can quit if they are unhappy. Rival companies compete for the same customers, and the player can buy shares in them. A company whose money runs out, or whose rating stays low for too long, loses the game, and the player starts again.

The towns

Fort Dunmore is an army town, with an old stone fort, cannons on display that are never fired, and soldiers at drill on the parade ground. Cedar Falls holds a fair with a parade led by a police car.

What is not in the game

  • No violence or injuryNo fighting, no weapons used, no blood, and nobody shown hurt.
  • No bad languageThe writing is clean, and the humor is gentle: a psychic who offers to forecast claims, a car hit by a meteorite.
  • No alcohol, tobacco or drugsNobody in the game drinks, smokes or takes drugs.
  • No sexual content and no horror
  • No ads and no in-app purchasesNothing in the game can be bought, and nothing asks for money.
  • No strangersThere is no chat, no online play, no shared content and no web browsing. The game never connects to the internet.
  • No data collectedThe game keeps your saved game on your phone and sends nothing anywhere. See the privacy policy.
